# Call of Slop: player manual, archived modes

The arena modes, Zombies and Co-op sections of the player manual, moved verbatim from docs/MANUAL.md on 2026-11-11. These modes are archived: their code stays in the game, nothing new is built on them.

### Matches

- These are the **Free-for-all** rules (the default). The host can pick another game mode; see *Game modes*.
- **First to 11 kills wins, but you must be 2 kills clear** of the next player (like tennis). At 11-10 the match carries on until someone leads by two.
- **Score limit: Auto (default) or Fixed** (Single player screen under the game mode, and the lobby room's MATCH section). Auto scales the limit with the number of players (people + bots) so a 24-player match takes about as long as an 8-player one: 11 kills is the limit at 8 players, more above that, fewer below (never under 7). It's set from everyone present when the match starts and only ever goes **up** if people join mid-match (never down when they leave). Fixed keeps the classic limits (11 kills, 40 team kills, 150 points, 3 captures). The match bar always shows the current limit. Formulas and measurements: [docs/archive/SCORE-SCALING.md](SCORE-SCALING.md). Saved in `settings.txt` as `score_limit`.
- **Warmup and ready-up** before every match (the first one and each one after the map vote): see *Ready-up* below.
- **5-second countdown** once everyone is ready: everyone is frozen (you can still look around), the loadout menu opens, and whatever you have selected deploys automatically at GO.
- **10 minutes max** (the host can pick 5, 10, 15, 20 or 30 in the lobby room; saved as `match_minutes`). When the clock runs out, the kill leader wins; a tie at the top is a draw.
- The clock and the score (you vs. the best other player) sit at the top of the screen. It turns orange with "Win by 2!" when the match goes to deuce.
- At the end you get VICTORY / DEFEAT / DRAW and the final scoreboard. Nobody can be hurt on that screen, and a new match starts on the same map after 12 seconds.
- Same rules in single player and multiplayer; the host's game decides when a match ends.

### Ready-up

- Every match opens with a **warmup**. The loadout screen opens; you can pick a loadout, respawn and walk around, but nobody can be hurt or shoot. A panel on the left lists everyone with a tick for who's ready.
- Press **F3** (rebindable: *Ready up*) or click **READY UP** in the loadout screen footer or the panel. The 5-second countdown starts as soon as **every person** is ready. Bots are always ready.
- **Single player:** Ready, Deploy or Respawn starts the countdown straight away.
- **Sandbox has none of this:** no ready-up and no countdown, in single player, hosting or joining. The host's START in the lobby room puts everyone in the world at once, and anyone joining later drops straight in.
- **Hosting:** the panel has a **FORCE START** button. The lobby room (LOBBY AND CHAT) also has *Auto-start once everyone is ready* (off by default): when it's on, the match starts that many seconds after the first person readies up, even if others haven't.
- When the countdown starts, everyone is put back at a spawn point. People who join during the warmup start not ready. People who join mid-match, or who leave, never hold anyone up.
- The warmup panel also shows the **map and mode**, and in team modes the players are listed under a **BLUE / RED** heading each.

### Game modes

Pick the mode on the Single player screen or in the lobby room. It stays the same from match to match (the map vote only changes the map), and the server browser, the match bar and the results screen show it. Every mode but Zombies, Co-op and Test Range has the same 10-minute limit.

| Mode | Rules |
| --- | --- |
| **Free-for-all** | First to 11 kills (at 8 players; see *Score limit* above), win by 2. |
| **Team Deathmatch** | Two teams (Blue and Red), pick your side or let the game balance them (see *Picking teams* below), bots included. First team to **40 kills** at 8 players; with the Auto score limit it grows with the player count (70 at 16, 120 at 32). No friendly fire, grenades and streaks included (your own grenade can still hurt you). You spawn on your team's side of the map. |
| **King of the Hill** | Two teams. A glowing ring marks the hill. Hold it with **only your team** inside for **1 point a second**; while both teams are in it (contested) nobody scores. The hill moves to the next zone every **60 s**. First to **150 points**. |
| **Capture the Flag** | Two teams, a flag at each base. Touch the enemy flag to take it; bring it to **your own flag** to capture, which only works while your flag is at home. A carrier who dies drops it; a teammate touching a dropped flag sends it home, otherwise it returns by itself after **20 s**. Carriers keep their guns, and a light beam over them (and over a dropped flag) shows through walls. First to **3 captures**. |
| **Gun Game** | Everyone starts with the same gun, the first one in the unlock order, whatever they've unlocked. Each kill moves you to the next gun; a **melee kill also sets the victim back one gun**. The first kill with the last gun wins (a melee kill on the last gun doesn't count). Loadouts, grenades, perks and kill streaks are off. The ladder is 20 guns, evenly spaced through the 50-odd weapons in unlock order. |
| **Zombies** | Co-op for 1-4 against endless rounds of zombies (World at War style), only on maps with a Zombies layout (**Blackmoor Asylum** in the Zombies pack, and the dev maps **Zombies Test** and **Horde Test**). No time limit: the run ends when everyone is down. See "Zombies" below. |
| **Co-op** | A squad of 1-4 (up to 6 with bots) plays a **mission** against waves of enemy soldiers, only on maps with a mission (today **World Tour: Silo**, mission *Silo Breach*). Objectives in order (kill, blow things up, hold a point, extract); a wipe restarts the objective at its checkpoint. No time limit. See "Co-op (PvE)" below. |
| **Test Range** | Free-for-all with **no win condition**: no score limit, no time limit, no results screen, no map vote, no map rotation. Bots respawn forever and you keep shooting them on the same map, 11 kills or 111, until you change it. The scoreboard still counts kills and deaths; the match bar says **TEST RANGE**. Your loadout, perks, grenades and kill streaks apply, and kill XP and unlocks count as usual (there's no end-of-match XP, since the match never ends). Plays on every normal map, single player or hosted. **Mid-session (Esc > HOST):** the host changes the bot count and difficulty on the spot (APPLY: extra bots spawn into the match, a new difficulty replaces every bot), and **BACK TO LOBBY** takes everyone to the lobby room, still connected, to pick another map, mode or bots and start again. Single player has no lobby room: leave the match to change the map. If a session ends at 11 kills with a results screen and a new map, it is not in Test Range: check the mode on the Single player screen or in the lobby room (the match bar reads **TEST RANGE**, the match log opens with "Test Range: no score or time limit"). |

Team modes colour everyone blue or red (players, scoreboard, kill card). King of the Hill is played by teams. The HUD shows the hill's holder, its distance and direction and when it moves; the state of both flags; or your Gun Game weapon, the next one and the leader. The scoreboard groups players by team (or by gun level). If a player leaves and the teams end up two apart, a bot switches sides.

On dev-pack maps (Zombies Test) the mode list also offers **Last Man Standing** (one life each, the last one standing wins), the Dev-only template that shows how a mode is written. For developers: every mode is a module under `Source/Game/Modes/<Mode>/` (rules flags, lifecycle, bots, HUD and replicated state in one class), registered in `GameModeRegistry`; [Source/Game/Modes/README.md](../../Source/Game/Modes/README.md) explains how to add one.

**Picking teams (play with your friends).** In every team mode you can choose **Join Blue**, **Join Red** or **Auto**:
- **Where:** the **TEAMS** window beside the loadout screen (it opens by itself in the warmup; the **TEAMS** button in the loadout footer opens or hides it, also on the respawn screen and when you join a match in progress) and **Esc > TEAMS**. Both show each team's roster, so you can see where your friends are; **Join** next to a person on the other side puts you on their team. A `*` after a name (also on the scoreboard) means they picked their team.
- **Size rule:** a side may be at most **2 players bigger** than the other (host setting *Max team size difference*, 0-4). Only people who picked a side count: bots and people on Auto fill the smaller side, so a group of friends can stack a little but not 6v2. A full side's button is greyed out with the reason. **Bots never block you:** join a side that has a bot and that bot moves to the other side.
- **Switching:** before the match (warmup, countdown, results) or while dead, at most once every **10 s**, never while carrying the flag. It's a respawn, not a suicide: no death, no score or kill streak change. While alive in a live match the buttons say to switch while dead.
- **Autobalance** only moves bots and people on **Auto** (people only while dead or between matches). Someone who picked a team is never moved; a new match keeps everyone's pick. People who join get the smaller side on Auto.
- **Team balance** (host setting: the Single player screen, the lobby room's TEAMS section and Esc > TEAMS; saved as `team_balance=auto|off|humans_vs_bots` and `humans_team=0|1`; older files get Auto):
  - **Auto** (default): everything above.
  - **Off:** no autobalance and no size limit on picks, so a group of friends can all stack one side; bots stay wherever they were put (a new match still splits people on Auto and bots evenly, then nobody is moved).
  - **Humans vs Bots:** every person on one side (pick **People on Blue** or **People on Red**, default Blue), every bot on the other; the bot slider sets how many bots you face. People who join mid-match go to the people's side, new bots to the bots' side; picking the bots' side is refused, and autobalance, SHUFFLE and BALANCE are off. Score limits, team spawns and map player limits work as in any team match.
- **Host settings** (the Single player screen and the lobby room's TEAMS section, shown for team modes; the host can also change them in Esc > TEAMS): **Team selection: Manual / Auto only** (default Manual; Auto only is the old automatic split) and **Max team size difference** (default 2). Saved in `settings.txt` as `team_select=manual|auto` and `team_max_diff=2`; older files get the defaults. Online, a client asks the server, which checks the rules and answers (protocol 20).

Maps can place team spawns, hills and flags by hand; otherwise the game works them out from the map (see Maps/README.md).

### Attachments (gunsmith)

Every weapon has up to six attachment slots: **Optic, Muzzle, Barrel, Underbarrel, Magazine, Stock**. Open a gun's attachments from the loadout screen: the **Edit attachments** button on the Primary or Secondary card, or **Attachments** (top right) on the weapon select. The attachments page shows the gun large, with how many attachments are unlocked and what's equipped; click a slot card to pick from it. Hover an attachment to see its pros, cons and the resulting stats.

- **Optics:** Pinhole Reflex (1.4x), Red Dot (1.5x), Holographic (1.6x), Brick 2x Prism, 2.5x ACOG, Tandem 3x Hybrid, 4x Scope, Variant 6x LPVO, 8x Sniper Scope and Longshot 12x (snipers only). Each has its own model, reticle and zoom, and the bigger the optic, the slower you aim.
- **Seeing through optics:** every optic is a physical sight on top of the gun. When you hold right click the gun comes up and the sight moves to your eye, housing and all, and you look through its lens: inside it you see only the world and a crisp reticle (no rail, receiver or tube insides), with a faint glass tint. The higher the magnification, the more of the screen the lens fills: reflex sights (Pinhole, Red Dot, Holographic) stay a small window on the gun, the 2x-3x prisms fill a good part of the screen, the 4x and 6x most of it, and the 8x and 12x all of it but a thin eyepiece rim in the corners. It's one continuous motion from the hip to full aim, with no switch to a flat overlay. Guns with a built-in scope work the same way. Each optic's look (reticle, how much of the screen its lens fills, rim, tint) is in its file's `sight` block ([Attachments/README.md](../../Attachments/README.md#sight-block)). Fitting an optic removes iron sights and LMG carry handles that would be in its way.
- **Snipers start scoped; iron sights are an unlock.** Every sniper rifle comes with a magnified scope fitted and unlocked from the start (Needle Bolt-Action, Leviathan .50 and Railspike the 8x Sniper Scope; Kite Semi-Sniper and Ghost SD the 4x Scope), so aiming zooms right away. Its **iron sights** (the empty optic slot: 1.5x, quicker to aim and lighter than any scope) unlock half-way through that gun's attachment XP (3,300-3,450 weapon XP, depending on how many attachments the gun takes), shown locked in the Optic list until then. Bots' snipers always use the scope. Older saves: a sniper kit with no optic (which used to look through a built-in scope) now gets the gun's starting scope; `loadout.txt` saves a chosen iron sight as `Iron Sights` in the kit. Other classes still start with iron sights.
- **Muzzle:** Suppressor (much quieter shots, faint tracers, -15% range), Compensator (cuts the climb), Muzzle Brake (cuts sideways recoil and shake), Choke (shotguns).
- **Barrel:** Long, Short, Heavy. **Underbarrel:** Vertical Grip (cuts the climb), Angled Grip (faster aim, softer first shot), Laser.
- **Magazine:** Extended, Fast, Drum. **Stock:** Light, Heavy (less recoil, faster recovery), No Stock.
- The gunsmith's stat bars show each change in green or red with the percentage. However much you stack, a gun keeps at least 40% of its recoil and half its hip spread.
- Which attachments fit depends on the weapon class, and every class has some of its own: pistols get the Slide Dot, slide comp, extended and snub barrels and a brace or holster stock; shotguns chokes, a slug barrel, a drum and a shell holder; snipers the 12x, a match barrel, a straight-pull action and stripper clips; LMGs a quick-change barrel and a long belt box; and the specials (nailgun, arc carbine, musket, toy blaster) their own sights, muzzles, barrels, gyro and braces, down to a blunderbuss bell and a **bayonet** for the musket (the bayonet makes a melee stab reach 3.2 m instead of 2.4 m). Every attachment is a trade (each makes at least one stat worse), and the full class-by-attachment table is in [Attachments/README.md](../../Attachments/README.md#which-classes-take-what). Ammunition (AP, incendiary) only changes stats.
- **Unlocks are per weapon** with weapon XP (see Progression): 3-7 matches with a gun to unlock everything on it. Weapon XP is saved in `progress.txt`, and Reset level clears it.
- The server applies the same attachments to the same stats, so they're fully networked. Other players hear your suppressor and see your optic and grip.

### Loadouts

Open **LOADOUTS** from the main menu, or press **B** in a match. The screen lists your loadout slots on the left. Pick one to make it the loadout you spawn with; it's marked with an orange star. The selected loadout is shown on the right as cards: **Primary Weapon**, **Secondary Weapon**, **Perks**, **Lethal**, **Tactical** and **Kill Streaks** (your hat is on the [Appearance](../MANUAL.md#appearance) screen now). Click a card to change that part, and **Rename** to name the loadout. Each loadout keeps its own attachments, and a gun you move into another loadout starts with the build you last used on it. Everything saves to `loadout.txt`. An older single-loadout file becomes slot 1.

- **More slots unlock as you level up.** Level 1 gets one slot, and the tenth arrives at max level, evenly spaced: levels 1, 6, 11, 17, 22, 27, 32, 38, 43 and 48. Only unlocked slots are listed, with a small "Next slot at level N" line under them. A locked slot keeps whatever you saved in it (after a reset or prestige, say) and comes back as it was once you reach its level again; if your selected slot is locked, you spawn with your highest unlocked one.
- **Back goes back one page.** The screen is a stack of pages: the footer's **BACK** button (and **Esc**) returns from the attachments to the weapon select (or to the overview, if you came from a card's Edit attachments), from the weapon select, lethal, tactical, perks and kill streak pages to the overview, and from the progression page to whatever was open before. On the overview, BACK returns to the main menu (in a match it's **CLOSE** and closes the menu). A line at the top shows where you are, e.g. *Custom Loadout 1 / Primary weapon / Kestrel PDW attachments*.
- **Your progression is the bar at the bottom right:** LEVEL, XP into the level and the next unlock. Click it (*ALL UNLOCKS*) for the progression page: level, XP, stats and every unlock by level.
- **Edit attachments:** the Primary and Secondary cards have an **Edit attachments** button beside the gun's name, which opens that gun's attachments directly. Clicking anywhere else on the card opens the weapon select.
- **Picking a gun:** clicking the Primary or Secondary card opens the weapon select. Class tabs run across the top (each shows how many guns it has and how many are still locked). Below, a column of tiles shows each gun with this loadout's attachments on it, its level (attachments unlocked) and weapon XP bar, and an EQUIPPED, SELECTED or LOCKED tag (with the level it unlocks at). **Click a tile to select it**: the gun is shown big on the right (hovering a tile previews it too), with its stats compared to the gun you have equipped (green = better, red = worse), its magazine, reload time and shots to kill. The selected tile has a blue outline; hover is orange, and the equipped gun has an orange bar and the EQUIPPED tag. **EQUIP** (bottom right) puts the shown gun in the loadout; it reads EQUIPPED for your current gun and is greyed out for a locked one. **Double-clicking a tile does exactly the same as EQUIP** (the tile flashes, then shows EQUIPPED); on a locked gun it just shakes.
- **Attachments on the weapon select** (top right) opens the attachments of the gun shown. For a gun you haven't equipped yet, the page opens as a preview, and picking your first attachment equips the gun into this loadout with that attachment. It's greyed out for locked guns.
- **Kill streaks:** the Kill Streaks card opens the streak page. Your three slots are along the top, and every streak is below with its kill cost, what it does, and the level it unlocks at if it's still locked. Click a streak to carry it; when all three slots are full, it replaces the highlighted slot (click a slot to choose which). Click a streak you carry to drop it. See [Kill streaks](#kill-streaks).
- **Hats** moved off the loadout screen to main menu > **APPEARANCE** (see [Appearance](../MANUAL.md#appearance)).
- **Double-click always means "pick it"** across the loadout screen (lethal and tactical lists, perks, kill streaks, attachment lists), and the second click of a double-click never also clicks whatever the page change put under the mouse. Clicking something locked gives a dry click.

### Perks

Each loadout carries one perk from each of three categories. Every perk only works in one situation, but it's strong when that situation comes up. Perks light up on the HUD (under your health) while they're active, and a banner announces the big moments.

Movement perks run inside the shared movement code (client prediction and the server agree). The server applies survival and damage perks, and bots carry perks that suit their personality. Handling perks (Sleight of Hand, Steady Aim, Tight Grip, Stalker) work on your own client like Overdrive does, and bots get Sleight of Hand, Steady Aim, Stalker and Lightweight too. Saved loadouts store perks by name, so older `loadout.txt` files load unchanged; the original perks keep their unlock levels.

**Last Stand in detail.** Once per life, the first bullet or blast that would kill you leaves you on 1 HP and raises a shield that blocks the **next 3 bullet hits** completely. One shot counts as one hit, so a shotgun blast uses a single charge however many pellets land, and every turret or drone bullet is a hit. **Melee and explosions go straight through the shield** and don't use charges: a knife or a grenade finishes off someone on Last Stand. Last Stand never saves you from a melee hit at all: melee kills in one hit. The shield lasts until its 3 charges are used or **8 seconds** pass, whichever comes first (so it can't carry over once health regeneration has refilled you). Three pips next to your health bar show the charges left. Everyone else sees a faint blue shell around a shielded player that flares each time it blocks a hit, with its own zap sound. Bots with Last Stand get exactly the same thing.

### Kill streaks

Each loadout carries **three kill streaks**. Kills in one life count up; reaching a streak's cost earns it, and an earned streak is **kept (even through death) until you use it**. Dying resets the count. Kills made *by* streaks never count toward the next one, and neither do kills after you've died (a grenade going off later, say). A streak kill gives normal kill XP.

Use an earned streak with its key: **4**, **5** and **6** by default, cheapest streak first (rebind them as *Kill streak 1-3*). Instant streaks go at once. The **Mortar Barrage**, **Strafe Run**, **Napalm Strike** and **Smoke Screen** start targeting: a marker shows where they'll land, **fire** calls them in, and aim or the streak key again cancels.

- **Everyone sees it coming.** A banner names whoever called in what ("Brick called in a Mortar Barrage", red when it's an enemy's), strikes put a red flare and danger marks where they'll land, and the turret and drone carry a light: green for yours, red for an enemy's.
- **Turrets, drones and gunships can be destroyed** with bullets, grenades and enemy strikes (no headshot bonus on machines). A Supply Drop crate can't: it's taken, not destroyed. Your own bullets pass through yours. The kill feed shows who destroyed what. A health bar floats over yours and your team's at all times (over an enemy's once it's damaged), and while yours is up your HUD shows its health and seconds left above the streak icons.
- **The HUD** shows your three streaks at the bottom centre: key, kill cost, a pip per kill and a glow when it's ready, plus your current streak count. A banner announces each one you earn.
- **Bots** carry three random streaks and use them: mortars, strafe runs and napalm on the last enemy they saw or the biggest group, turrets facing where the enemies are, a smoke wall halfway to an enemy they've just seen, the rest straight away. Insane squads use the same logic.
- **Zombies has no kill streaks** (nor does Gun Game): the mode turns them off, so none of these are in it. The co-op mode has its own power-ups and perks instead.
- **For other weapons that counter streaks** (e.g. launchers): every streak machine is a `StreakEntity` with `IsAirborne` (Attack Drone, Hunter Drone, Gunship), `Health` / `MaxHealth`, `Hitbox` and `Targetable` (false for a crate). `Game.DamageStreakMachine(entity, attacker, amount, DamageKind, now)` applies a direct hit or blast on the server through the normal rules (team check, the streak's bullet and explosive multipliers, the kill-feed line), and `Game.StreakMachineHostile(entity, player)` says whether that player may target it. Grenade and strike blasts already hurt them through `StreakBlastEntities`.
- Streaks unlock with your level like perks: three to start, the others spread evenly. They're saved in `loadout.txt` (`loadout1.streaks=Recon Sweep, Mortar Barrage, Sentry Turret`); older files get the three starters, and a locked streak is swapped for a starter.
- Everything is decided on the server (counts, activation, damage). Strikes (mortar, strafe, napalm, smoke) are planned from one random seed, so clients draw the shells, fire and smoke without extra messages; turrets, drones, gunships and crates send 22 bytes each with every snapshot; a gunship round or Hunter Drone blast is one 24-byte event. Armour rides in your own streak-state message (12 bytes since protocol 29, which also made the earned-streak bits 16 wide). Streaks 9 and up use kill-feed ids 214-229, so weapon ids stop at 213.

### Launchers

**Launchers are secondaries** (you carry one instead of a pistol) that fire real projectiles: rockets and grenades fly, drop, can be dodged and go off on contact, with a blast that works like a grenade's (walls shield you, Flak Jacket and going prone help, your own blast hurts you, Demolitions widens it, blasts push people and throw ragdolls). They're the counter to kill streaks: every launcher hits turrets, drones, the Gunship and other streak machines hard, and the **Skylance AA locks on**: aim down its sights at an enemy's flying streak and hold it in the sight; a tone ticks faster as the lock builds, goes solid and a red **LOCKED** bracket closes on the target; fire and the rocket homes in. Ground streaks (the sentry) never lock and take dumb fire. Ammo is limited: the magazine plus a few spare rounds per life (shown as `magazine | spare`), refilled when you respawn.

| Launcher | Type | vs players: damage (centre) / blast radius / direct-hit bonus, kills within | vs objects and machines: damage / radius | Rounds (mag + spare) | vs streaks | Unlock |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mule RPG | dumb-fire rocket, slow, big blast | 202 / 8.5 m / +81, 3.2 m | 150 / 6.5 m | 1 + 2 | sentry in 2 direct hits, drone in 1 (if you hit it) | level 6 |
| Clover Break-Action | single-shot grenade launcher, arcing | 176 / 6.6 m / +54, 1.8 m | 130 / 5 m | 1 + 5 | sentry in 3 | level 9 |
| Carousel Drum Launcher | 6-round revolving grenade launcher (bounces once) | 130 / 5.5 m / +39, 0.3 m | 100 / 4.2 m | 6 + 6 | sentry in 3 | level 14 |
| Skylance AA | lock-on anti-air rocket, proximity fuse | 143 / 4.6 m / +182, 0.5 m | 110 / 3.5 m, x1.3 vs machines | 1 + 3 | Attack / Hunter Drone in 1 lock-on hit, Gunship in 2 (3 rockets with its flare); sentry in 2 dumb-fire hits | level 18 |
| Gluepot Sticky Launcher | sticky charges (stick to walls and people), remote detonated | 169 / 6 m / -, 1.4 m | 125 / 4.5 m | 3 + 3 | sentry in 3 | level 22 |
| Wireline TOW | wire-guided rocket: it flies where you look while you hold it | 189 / 6.6 m / +108, 2.1 m | 140 / 5 m | 1 + 2 | sentry in 2 | level 27 |

| Furnace Thermobaric | wide blast, slow falloff, half of it gets round walls | 156 / 10.5 m / +52, 2.9 m | 120 / 8 m | 1 + 1 | sentry in 3 | level 39 |

Launcher blasts hit players about a third harder and wider than they hit things: every launcher's centre kills a 120 HP player, and "kills within" is how close the blast alone (in the open, standing) is lethal. Props, buildings, destructible structures and kill streak machines take the second column, so lock-on hit counts and how many rockets a wall takes are unchanged. Your own blast hurts you by the same player numbers (60% of them), so every round arms far enough out that one going off at its arming distance takes under half your health.

- **Arming**: a round flies a few metres before it arms (the Mule RPG 8 m, the Clover 5 m, the Carousel 4 m, a Gluepot charge 3 m); an unarmed hit is a dud that only does its (unbuffed) direct-hit damage. Rockets self-destruct after their fuse, grenades go off at theirs.
- **Sticky launcher**: hip-fire sticks a charge to whatever it hits (up to the magazine's three out at once); **aim and pull the trigger** to set them all off. The HUD shows how many you have out.
- **Wire guidance**: the rocket turns towards whatever is under your crosshair for as long as you keep the launcher in hand (switch away and the wire is cut); a "GUIDING" ring shows while one is on the wire.
- **Flares**: the Gunship carries one flare: it pops it when the first locked rocket closes in (45 m), and that rocket chases the flare instead, so a Gunship takes 2 locked Skylance hits (3 rockets with the flare). Attack and Hunter Drones have none.
- **Attachments**: launchers take only their own five: Thermal Tube Sight (2x, green tint), Quick-Lock Module (-30% lock time, +15% lock range), Spare Round (+1 round), Long Tube (+20% launch speed), Shoulder Pad (faster aim, less kick). Nothing made for guns fits a launcher.
- **Not in Gun Game** (the ladder is guns only) **and not in Zombies** (its weapons are built from parts; launchers aren't wall-buys or in the box).
- **Bots**: about a quarter of the bots on Normal and up (four in ten tacticians) carry a launcher. They bring it out for an enemy streak machine they can see (locking flying ones with the Skylance) and, on Hard and up, for enemies bunched together at a safe distance, then go back to their main gun.
- **Netcode**: the host simulates every projectile. A 38-byte spawn message starts it everywhere, each client flies its own copy with the same deterministic steps, guided and stuck ones get a 29-byte correction with the snapshots, and a 25-byte message ends it with the server's result. Your own rocket appears the moment you fire (predicted) and the server's copy takes over without a jump; a new rocket is flown forward by the shooter's latency (up to 100 ms) so it is where the shooter saw it. Protocol 30.
- **Modding**: launchers are weapon files in `Weapons/Launchers` with a `"launcher"` block (projectile, guidance, speeds, blast, arming, fuses, lock-on, cluster, thermobaric; see [Weapons/README.md](../../Weapons/README.md#launchers)). New flying kill streaks register how launchers treat them (`LauncherTargets.Register`: airborne, lock-on hits to kill, flares).

### Bots

Bots play through exactly the same input commands as people (movement, mantling, stances, shooting, grenades, melee), so they follow the same rules. Difficulty (**Easy, Normal, Hard, Insane**) sets reaction time, aim error and turn speed ([Insane](#insane-bots-squads) also makes them play as squads). On top of that every bot has a **personality**, shown after its name, e.g. `[BOT] Brick (Rusher)`. A lobby gets a mix:

| Personality | Plays like |
|---|---|
| Rusher | Hunts the nearest enemy and fights up close with SMGs and shotguns; fast, twitchy strafing; barely retreats. |
| Marksman | Holds long sightlines with DMRs and snipers from [vantage points](#long-range-bots-vantage-points) (rooftops, windows, hills), crouches or goes prone; moves on after a kill or two. |
| Flanker | Takes side routes to get behind people (the path search avoids the direct line); commits to one strafe direction. |
| Anchor | Holds an open spot (or the objective) with rifles and LMGs, crouch-peeks, uses cover a lot, and only chases nearby. |
| Tactician | More grenades (including at where you ducked out of sight), pre-aims corners, retreats to cover to reload. |
| Chaotic | Any gun, any habit. |

Personalities also change engagement range, aggression, retreat health, strafing rhythm, cover use and perks (in Gun Game everyone gets the ladder's gun and the shared perks, so only the habits differ). In King of the Hill and Capture the Flag, each life a bot rolls whether it plays the objective or hunts, by personality: Anchors almost always go for it, Tacticians often, Rushers rarely. Bots navigate with a **waypoint graph** built for each map the first time it's played (a slice per tick, usually within a second or two; until then they steer straight): nodes on every surface you can stand on, linked where a player can really walk, step up, mantle (up to 1.7 m) or drop, checked with the game's own collision. They plan routes with A\*, cut corners when the way ahead is clear, climb ledges, step aside for each other in doorways, and re-plan when they get stuck. **Doors:** a bot opens a shut building door on its route with the same interact the F key does (same reach, in plain view, at most one press every 0.3 s) about 2 m before it gets there, so it walks straight through without stopping; one body goes through a doorway at a time (it waits a moment for someone coming the other way). After walking through, a bot may shut the door behind it if nobody is following (Anchors and Tacticians usually do, Rushers almost never); a bot falling back to cover or in an Insane squad's retreat always does. A bot holding a spot inside a building shuts that room's door (walking over to it first) and stays inside while it holds. It never shuts a door on a teammate who is coming through, never one its own route goes back through, and never moves one door more than twice in 10 s, so bots don't fight over doors or flap them open and shut. Nobody can shut a door on someone standing in the doorway (players, bots, co-op enemies or zombies). Co-op enemies open doors the same way and may shut one from cover against the players beyond it. Zombies bash shut building doors down (40 damage a swipe, so 4 swipes for a wooden door). Their own Zombies-mode doors stay barriers they never open. A bot that stays stuck escalates: after about 3 s it hops and backs off, after 6 s it avoids the spot, drops its goal and heads for the nearest walkable spot, and after 15 s (or 4 s off the walkable area, e.g. in a pit with no way out) it uses the same **Kill yourself** as the pause menu: a suicide, no kill for anyone, normal respawn. Holding a spot, the hill or a squad position, fighting, warmup, countdown and the results screen never count. Modes without respawns teleport instead (Zombies co-op bots join the nearest standing teammate; Last Man Standing goes to the nearest walkable spot). Each one is logged to `callofslop.log` as `[bots] stuck: ...` with the map, position and cause (once a minute per bot), and every match end logs the count for the map, so map trouble spots can be found and fixed. In a fight they keep to their weapon's range, strafe, duck behind cover to reload or when hurt, then peek back; out of a fight they chase where they last saw you, investigate gunfire, and glance around corners. They aim at the chest and drag their aim back down under recoil, so headshots are a minority of their kills.

**Bot gear matches the players' level.** By default (host setting **Bot gear: Match players**, on the Single player screen and in the lobby room's BOTS section, saved as `bot_gear=` in `settings.txt`) bots only carry what a player of the lobby's level could have unlocked: weapons, launchers, attachments, perks, grenades, tactical equipment and kill streaks. The lobby's level is the average account level of the people in the game (bots don't count; anyone who has prestiged counts as the max level; offline it's your own level), worked out again whenever someone joins or leaves and at every bot life. Each bot sits up to 3 levels either side of it (fixed per bot, so they differ), but never above a level-1 lobby: new players face bots with the starter guns, starter perks and starter streaks, no grenade and no equipment, just like their own loadout. Attachments follow weapon XP: a bot is taken to have levelled a gun over the 6 account levels after it unlocks, so a fresh unlock is bare and an older gun is fully kitted. Personalities still choose by class among what's unlocked (a Marksman takes the DMR at level 1 and snipers once they unlock). **Everything** gives bots the whole arsenal (as before), **Starter** gives every bot level-1 gear. Gun Game's ladder, the shared Gun Game perks and Zombies' own builds work as before. Bots never wear prestige hats.

**Bots and foliage.** Bots can't see through foliage, the way you can't: grass, bushes, hedges of bushes, shrubs and tree canopies hide you from them (tree trunks and the solid hedgerow props already block like walls), and so do cornfields. Bullets still go straight through plants. It goes by stance: lying in tall grass you're hidden from a bot 15 m away, but standing or crouching in knee-high grass your head shows above it and they see you; a crouch behind a chest-high bush hides you, standing behind it doesn't. Within 2.5 m a bot sees you whatever is in between. Hiding isn't silent: running near a bot (about 14 m, not with Dead Silence) or shooting gives your rough position away. When a bot knows roughly where you are in the cover (it saw you go in, heard you, or, for Insane squads, a squadmate who sees you called your spot), it may **fire blind** into the cover at its guess: short, slower bursts with a cone five times its normal spread around a spot that gets less accurate the longer it's been since it last knew anything, never at your head, and it gives up about 3 s after its last information. It never knows where you really are, so crawling away from where you were seen works. Only bots are affected (people see the plants on screen), the bots use every plant whatever your Foliage setting is, and none of it changes movement, bullets or the map's fingerprint. Under the hood the map's plants are rasterised once per map into a 1 m grid of cover layers (grass, brush, canopy) on a worker thread, and a sight line adds up the metres of foliage it crosses (1 m of dense leaves hides you) in about a microsecond (`Map.Concealed`, Source/Maps/FoliageCover.cs; Source/Game/BotFoliage.cs).

**How players and bots look.** Everyone shares one blocky body in the style of the legs and arms: a shirt with chest, belly and shoulders that meet the arms, a plate carrier (front and back plates, cummerbund, mag pouches and a patch in your team colour), a collar and neck, and a head with jaw, brow, nose, eyes and ears. Each name gets its own seeded look, the same on every machine and every match: skin tone, hair colour, a beard or not, and headwear (hair, a buzz cut, a helmet, a patrol cap or a team-colour bandana). Bots pick headwear by personality: Anchors always wear helmets, Tacticians mostly, Marksmen caps, Rushers bandanas or buzz cuts. A prestige hat replaces your headwear and sits on the head exactly as before. Zombies get the same treatment: a torn shirt over a bare belly, a slack jaw and a heavy brow. It's cosmetic only: hit boxes and the headshot line are unchanged. Beyond 30 m the small details (pouches, patch, collar, eyes, ears) are skipped, and a body costs at most 1.5x the draws of the old box body.
### Long-range bots (vantage points)

Bots carrying a **sniper rifle or a DMR** (an LMG sometimes; a launcher only while an enemy aircraft is up) prefer to **find a good vantage point before they engage**. Each life a bot rolls whether it plays this way, by personality and difficulty: Marksmen nearly always, Anchors and Tacticians often, Rushers and Flankers sometimes, and Easy bots rarely.

- **Vantage points** are worked out once per map, on a worker thread the first time bots play it (about the time the waypoint graph takes; nothing in the map, its fingerprint or the network changes). Candidates are the waypoint nodes with the most height over their surroundings (rooftops, upper floors and windows, hills, ridges) or the most open view; each is scored per range band (DMR medium-long, sniper long, LMG medium) on its **sightlines** to the hill zones, flag stands and a ground-level spot in every part of the map (the routes people walk), its **cover** (a parapet or sandbag to duck behind and peek over, a roof edge, walls, a roof overhead; spots in the open lose points) and its **height**. The best few dozen per map are kept, at least 5 m apart, all reachable.
- **Getting there:** the bot heads for the best free spot that overlooks the action (the hill, the flag it's after, where it last saw or heard someone, else the nearest enemy) and that no teammate holds (in free-for-all, nobody) and that wasn't compromised lately. A Rusher with a sniper picks a closer, more aggressive spot. On the way it **holds its fire** at long range (beyond about 28 m with a sniper, 22 m with a DMR) unless it's shot at, targeted or the enemy is close.
- **Holding:** at the spot it ducks behind the spot's cover and stands up to peek, scans the directions the spot overlooks (or where it heard gunfire), and shoots from there, crouched or prone when that still sees the target, ducking to reload. It **relocates** after a couple of kills, when it's shot from a direction it isn't watching (that spot counts as compromised for 40 s), when the area goes quiet, or after 12-45 s, so a sniper never sits in one place all match.
- **Up close** (an enemy within 8 m) it gives the spot up and fights normally, and a bolt-action or other slow-firing rifle swaps to the **sidearm** (not a launcher), back to the rifle once nobody has been that close for a few seconds.
- **Modes and squads:** it works in Team Deathmatch, Free-for-all, Gun Game (with the ladder's gun) and, overwatching the hill or the flag, in King of the Hill and Capture the Flag (a flag carrier just runs). In an **Insane squad** the long-range member (sniper first, then DMR, then LMG; never the one leading) gets an **overwatch** order: hold a vantage point over the squad's goal while the others move. Zombies co-op bots don't use vantage points.

### Insane bots (squads)

**Insane** is Hard+ (faster reactions, tighter aim, faster turning) and, in team modes, the Insane bots on each team play as **squads of 3-4** (two bots make a pair; a bot alone plays solo). Each member has a role picked from its personality: a **leader** (Tacticians first) who picks where the squad goes, a **suppressor** (Marksmen, Anchors), a **flanker** (Flankers, Rushers) and, in a squad of four, a **support**. Squads share a **blackboard** of what their members have actually seen or heard (no wallhacks: an enemy nobody can see isn't on it, and contacts are forgotten after a few seconds), and plan together, one squad per server tick, about twice a second each:

- **Moving:** the squad heads for one shared goal (the mode's objective, the last contact, or a sweep towards the enemy's side) in a wedge around the leader. Near trouble it **bounds**: one pair moves 14 m ahead while the other holds, crouched, watching the way ahead, then they swap. If half the squad falls 25+ m behind, the leader waits and the rest **regroup**.
- **Attacking:** when a member spots you, the suppressor (and the leader) take spots with a line to where you were seen, keep their distance and keep you busy, with **suppressive fire** at your last known spot when you duck out of sight. The flanker goes round to your side or back (85-155 degrees off the suppressor's angle) on a route that avoids what you can see from where you were, waits at a staging point out of sight, and pushes when everyone is in place; it holds its fire until it has a good angle (unless you spot it). A support either sets up a **crossfire** from a second angle or, if it likes to flank, makes it a **pincer** from the other side. Their routes avoid each other, so they don't all come through one door. The push comes with a frag on your spot from someone who can't see you.
- **Trades and retreat:** a squadmate killed in view of you calls out your position and the nearest member comes to **trade** the kill. A squad of 3-4 down to its last member **retreats** towards its spawn, under smoke when the support carries it, and regroups.

In free-for-all, Insane bots are Hard+ individuals; in Zombies, Insane co-op bots spread their fire over different zombies and only the nearest one goes to revive a downed teammate while the others keep shooting. Pick Insane in the lobby's Difficulty row (single player and hosting); it's saved as `difficulty=Insane` in `settings.txt`, and older settings files load unchanged.

### Respawning

- **Loadouts only change between lives.** During a life you can still edit loadouts, and the changes apply the next time you spawn. The exceptions are the warmup and the 5-second pre-match countdown, when changes apply immediately.
- **Death cam and kill card.** When you die, the camera rises over your body, then turns to face whoever killed you and zooms in a little, following them for about 3 seconds. A **kill card** shows the killer's name, their gun with its attachments (optic marked), headshot, distance, how much health they had left and their streak (melee, grenades, streak rewards and killing yourself get their own versions). Click, or press the loadout key, to skip straight to the loadout screen. On wide screens the card stays next to the loadout window.
- **Kill notices.** When you kill someone, **KILLED <name>** appears under your crosshair in their colour (team colour in team modes), with tags under it: HEADSHOT, MELEE, the grenade or streak that did it (FRAG GRENADE, SENTRY TURRET...), LONGSHOT and the distance for gun kills from 40 m, DOUBLE / TRIPLE / QUAD KILL for kills within 2.5 s of each other (their lines stack), REVENGE on whoever killed you last, and every 5 kills in a row. It fades about 2 s after your last kill, next to the +XP popups. Only the killer sees it (not in Zombies); everyone's kill feed (top right) names both players and fades out.
- **Nobody is force-respawned.** When you die, the loadout screen opens after the death cam. Pick a loadout, then click **RESPAWN**. The button counts down the 3-second respawn delay, and clicking early queues it. You also start every session on this screen.
- Close the screen to just watch. Press the loadout key to reopen it.

### Map vote

At the end of every match the results screen shows **three maps to vote on**, taken from the pack of the map you just played (with **True Random**: from every pack but Dev; from every map if that pack is too small): one small, one medium and one large where possible, never the map you just played. Click one or press **1 / 2 / 3**. The mouse cursor stays free for the whole results screen (clicking empty space, or opening and closing the pause menu, doesn't capture it); it is captured again by itself when the next match's warmup or countdown starts, if the game window is focused and no menu is open. You can change your vote until the 12-second timer runs out, and the live tally shows which map is leading. Only maps big enough for the people playing are offered (see [Lobby size](../MANUAL.md#lobby-size)): a map's player limit in the current mode must be at least the number of **human** players here, counting the host and anyone still loading in; bots don't count (they leave a smaller map and come back on a bigger one). If fewer than three maps are big enough the ballot has fewer options; if none is, the map you just played is offered again when it is big enough, otherwise the largest maps (the server log says so). If someone joins during the vote and an option becomes too small, that option is swapped in its slot for another map that fits (votes for it are dropped, so those players vote again; other votes keep counting), or removed if no other map fits. The same rule applies if the winner can't be used (a map file removed meanwhile): a random map that fits is used instead. Only human players vote; bots never do and aren't counted. Once every human has voted, the timer drops to 3 seconds. The next match starts on the map with the most votes, with ties broken at random among the tied maps; if no human votes (say, a solo host who doesn't click), one of the three is picked at random. Players who join during the results screen see the ballot and can vote.

### Progression

You earn XP in **single player and multiplayer** alike. There are two tracks, both filled by the same play:

- **Account XP (levels):** +100 per kill, +50 more for a headshot, +25 more for a grenade kill, **+150 for finishing a match** and **+250 for winning it**. **Each level unlocks a weapon, grenade or equipment**, 48 levels in all. Perks, kill streaks and loadout slots unlock on top of that, spread evenly over the same levels. A level costs ~2,400-3,800 XP.
- **Weapon XP (attachments):** each gun earns its own XP: +100 per kill with it, +50 for a headshot, plus +150 per match for the primary you carried (+75 for the secondary). Its attachments unlock along a curve, with the first few quickly and the last ones slower.

The pacing is a rough target rather than a hard rule. A typical match is around 7 kills, which is ~1,000 XP on each track. So:

- **A gun is fully kitted out in 3-7 matches.** Guns with few attachment options (pistols) are on the short end, and guns with lots (rifles, DMRs) on the long end.
- **A new level, and with it a new weapon, comes at about the point your current gun is ~60% kitted.** That works out to roughly every 2-3 matches, so you get time with each gun before the next one shows up.

The main menu shows your level, XP bar, the PRESTIGE button and a one-line next unlock, kept to the width of the buttons (long text wraps rather than widening the menu). The XP values and the list of what every level unlocks live on the progression page: the level bar at the bottom of the loadout screen opens the progression page, which lists every level. In a match, XP pops up by the crosshair, and banners announce level-ups and new attachments. Progress is saved to `progress.txt`, with weapon XP stored by weapon name (`wx.<name>=...`). Older saves carry over: their numbered weapon XP is converted to names, and old per-weapon kill counts become weapon XP.

- **Level 1:** BR-1 Prototype, Vanguard AR, Hornet SMG, Breacher Pump, Marksman M14, P9 Sidearm, Mk.23 Tactical.
- **Levels 2-48:** the other 43 weapons plus the Frag Grenade (level 4), Recon Pulse (8), Impact Grenade (13) and Smoke Grenade (20), in a fixed order. The tactical equipment sits on top of that: Stim Shot (5), Flashbang (10), Decoy (13), Motion Sensor (16), Stun Grenade (19), Trophy System (24), Deployable Cover (28), Tactical Insertion (32). The fun ones come last: Grinder Rotary, Railspike, Toy Blaster.
- Locked items appear as `[LOCKED] ... level N` (weapons) or `[LOCKED] ... N weapon XP` (attachments). Bots ignore progression.
- Weapons added as files unlock at the level their file asks for (`unlockLevel`), or at level 48.
- Unlocks are kept on each player's own PC.

### Prestige

At max level (48) the lobby's **PRESTIGE** button (under the level bar; greyed out before max level) resets your account for a hat. A confirmation dialog says what resets and which hat you get ("Prestige 1: unlocks the Beanie"). There are five prestiges, one hat each:

| Prestige | Hat |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | **Beanie**: a small red knit beanie with a folded cuff and a bobble |
| 2 | **Baseball Cap**: blue crown, brim forward |
| 3 | **Fedora**: pinched crown with a black band and a wide brim |
| 4 | **Top Hat**: tall black cylinder with a red band |
| 5 | **Ridiculous Top Hat**: about 3.5 times as tall as the top hat (1.4 m), sways as you move |

- **What resets (the CoD rule):** level and account XP go back to level 1 / 0 XP, and every gun's weapon XP is cleared, so weapons, grenades, equipment, perks, kill streaks, loadout slots and attachments all lock again and are earned again. Loadouts fall back to unlocked items on their own (as after Reset level).
- **What you keep:** your hats and your lifetime stats (kills, headshots, matches, wins). Settings > Reset level keeps your prestige too.
- **Wearing a hat:** pick it on the main menu's **APPEARANCE** screen (None or any unlocked hat; locked ones show "Prestige N"). Others see it on your head in third person, in the death cam and kill cam, and lying by your corpse; you never see it in first person.
- **Cosmetic only:** hats have no collision and don't change your hitbox, and prestige gives nothing else. Bots never prestige and never wear hats.
- Your prestige shows as **P2** before your level: in the lobby, on the HUD level line and in the scoreboard's **Level** column (`P2 L13`, with the hat the player wears beside it; bots show `BOT`).
- Saved in `progress.txt` as `prestige=N`; older saves load as prestige 0. Hat models are data: `Cosmetics/hats.json` (format `call-of-slop-cosmetics/1`; boxes in head space, `"round"` parts made of a few turned boxes).
- **Network:** your client sends its prestige and level after its name in the Hello; the roster carries prestige, level, hat and [look](../MANUAL.md#appearance) for every player (6 bytes each, nothing in the snapshots). The server only uses the reported prestige for what it shows and to check the hat you pick (`Cosmetics.IsUnlocked`); it never affects gameplay. A level-up mid-match reaches everyone's scoreboard at your next respawn: the loadout packet carries your prestige and level too, and the server resends the roster only when they changed (nothing in the snapshots; older clients and servers simply skip the two bytes).

Every weapon has its own stats: damage and headshot multiplier, pellets, fire rate and mode (semi/burst/auto), magazine, reload, hip and aimed spread, recoil, damage falloff, movement speed, zoom and draw time.

**Recoil is per weapon.** Each gun has its own seeded spray pattern, so holding the trigger always traces the same learnable shape, plus jitter, a first-shot kick and stance and aim multipliers. Assault rifles climb steadily with a gentle drift. SMGs kick little per shot but jump around. LMGs climb hard, but crouched or prone they barely move sideways. DMRs snap once and settle fast, snipers and shotguns punch the camera, pistols flip, and the specials each have their own feel (the Arc Carbine snakes, the Grinder spins up). When you stop firing, the aim drifts most of the way back to where you started. The camera shake and punch are only visual and never move your aim. Each weapon file's `recoil` block holds the numbers (see [Weapons/README.md](../../Weapons/README.md)).

**Weapons and attachments are data files.** Each weapon is one `.json` file in the `Weapons` folder (one subfolder per class), and each attachment is one file in `Attachments`, holding its slot, which weapons it fits and the stat changes it makes. To add a weapon, drop a file in and restart. Its sounds are generated from its stats and its model from its `model` block (an archetype like `pump_shotgun` or `bolt_sniper` plus any changes to receiver, barrels, handguard, magazine, stock and sights), so every gun has its own silhouette, and it picks its own unlock level with `unlockLevel`. **[Weapons/README.md](../../Weapons/README.md)** and **[Attachments/README.md](../../Attachments/README.md)** are the guides, with an example and every field. A broken file is skipped with a message in the lobby, and `CallOfSlop.exe --check-weapon FILE` checks one without starting the game. Weapon XP and loadouts are saved by weapon name. In multiplayer everyone needs identical `Weapons` and `Attachments` folders: a client whose set differs from the host's is told which folder differs and disconnected.
The server checks everything: you can only fire weapons you own, at their real fire rate and spread, and you can't move faster than your fastest weapon allows.

### Zombies (in development)

A World at War style **Zombies** mode (co-op for 1-4, new maps) is being built. Milestone 1, the **modular weapon builder**, is playable now as a sandbox: main menu > **WORKBENCH (ZOMBIES PREVIEW)**.

- A weapon is a tree of **parts** (267 so far, in `Parts/`): the **grip** is the root and holds one or more **receivers** (a pistol grip 3, a rifle grip 9, up to 16 on the late-game frames and the push cart), and each receiver has its own **action**, barrels, **splitters**, feed, ammo, a **damage multiplier** slot and two **receiver upgrade** slots (behind and beside it). Stocks, optics, foregrips, a **shared feed** (one hopper feeding several receivers) and utility parts go on the grip. **The receiver picks the calibre; the action part on it picks the fire type and rate** (single shot, semi, burst, pump, lever, bolt, full auto, rotary, chain or muzzle-loaded: 28 actions, from a Break Action to a Panic Button). Builds saved before actions get each receiver's old action when they load.
- **Making parts:** parts are JSON files, and new ones can be a few lines: model **fragments** (`Parts/_Fragments`: rails, screws, fins, scope tubes, drums...) placed with `{"use": ..., "pos", "rot", "scale", "color"}`, and `"extends"` to start from a base or another part (`Parts/_Bases`); socket types and bores live in `Parts/_Registry.json`; `Parts/_Template` has a commented template. **`--watch-parts`** reloads parts as you save them (workbench preview and the guns in your hands in the range; the pause menu has *Reload part files*), and `--check-part FILE`, `--check-parts` `tools/parts/viz.py` (a picture of every part and its sockets) and `tools/parts/floaters.py` (model boxes floating free of the rest) check them. See [docs/sandbox/WEAPON-BUILDER.md](../sandbox/WEAPON-BUILDER.md#making-parts-fragments-bases-hot-reload-tools). In multiplayer everyone needs identical `Parts` folders.
- The **grip fixes the fire style**: volley grips fire every receiver at its own rate (its own trigger and magazine; the ready ones together), rotate grips take turns. **Splitters never change the total damage** (rarer ones are more accurate or have more outputs: rows, vertical stacks, grids, a cross, a six-barrel rosette, a 3 x 3 wall; **offset** splitters have one output and just move the barrel up, down, sideways or canted); damage comes from multipliers and upgrades. **Barrels change the round itself**: bigger and slower (Heavy Bore, Bloat Barrel, Cannon Sleeve: harder hits with a hit radius, so a fat slug clips what a thin bullet misses, and you can watch it travel) or smaller and faster (Squeeze Bore, Needle Barrel, Flechette Sleeve: more reach and wall penetration, less damage), and **muzzle devices** have big effects (the Recoil Eater halves the kick, the Dragon Mouth turns a rifle into a burning cone, the Fork Crown splits every round in two, the Long Choke tightens any gun's spread). **Weight** is the limit: rarer frames and weight-relief parts (slings, harnesses, an anti-grav coil, helium balloons) carry more, so huge guns come late in a run.
- **The power curve** (2026-10-27): every category has a rarity ladder, and the rarest parts are much stronger and come late. Receivers have a real damage spread (13 for the Peashooter up to 255 for the Bunker Buster and 360 for the Parade Cannon's cannon shells); the receivers that were only a fire rate (Ratchet Burst, Triad Burst, Buzzbox) are gone, and builds on them load on a Stampede, Workhorse or Plinker with the burst or buzzsaw action they fired with. 35 late-game parts: x3 and x5 damage chambers, a self-reloading sear, bowling-ball and pocket-sun rounds, a 999-round clown car hopper for sixteen receivers, a parade float frame, a jetpack stock, an optic that marks every zombie through walls, and melee heads (wrecking ball, swordfish, grandfather clock) and a jet engine that **launch** zombies across the room. Zombies drop legendary parts from round 18 and wonder parts from round 25; the slot machines pay epic at most before round 20 and legendary at most before 25, however hot the machine or big the bet. See "The power curve" in [docs/sandbox/WEAPON-BUILDER.md](../sandbox/WEAPON-BUILDER.md#the-power-curve-late-game-pass-2026-10-27).
- The **push cart** is a real cart: you push it along the ground with both hands on the bar (it can't climb steps or steep slopes, and it stops at walls), and it doesn't fire while you push it. **F parks it**; **tap F** at its bar to **man its gun** (you stand at the bar, the mouse turns the gun up to 60 degrees either side, 15 down and 30 up, and the fire key fires the cart's build); **F** (or jump / sprint) steps off back to your other weapon, and **holding F** at the cart pushes it again. (An auto-firing turret may come later as a separate deployable.)
- The **melee slot**: build a melee weapon from a hilt, a head and mods (machete, nail bat, sledgehammer, fire axe, chainsaw sword, a frying pan, a frozen fish...) with its own damage, swing speed, reach, cleave and stun. It replaces the knife only in the workbench range (and Zombies later); normal matches keep the standard melee.
- The workbench builds a gun in two steps. **New weapon** starts with **step 1, the grip**: cards with a 3D picture, how many receiver slots it has, how it fires, what it carries and how many you own (only the grips you own are listed; tick **Show all grips** to see every grip in the game, the ones you're missing dimmed, and locked at a Zombies bench; first-tier grips hold **one receiver**; grips with every count from 2 to 9 receivers come later, as rare and epic frames; a **Chain fire** checkbox on the build makes several receivers take turns instead of firing as a volley, saved with the build). **Step 2, assembly**: your parts on the left (grouped, counted, searchable, 3D icons; only parts that fit the build and have a spare copy are listed; tick **Show incompatible parts** to also list the rest, dimmed with the reason; off by default and remembered), the gun large in the middle (drag to turn it, wheel to zoom) and the build as a clean **linear list** (Grip > Receiver > Action > Feed > Ammo > Barrel > Muzzle > Stock > Optic, splitters indented) with live stat bars on the right. **Drag a part onto the gun**: the sockets it fits light up, it previews snapped in place, and snaps in on release; a part that doesn't fit bounces back with the reason. Swapping a part keeps what was on it where it still fits. **Every barrel fits every receiver**: the receiver sizes it (thinner and shorter on a pistol, fatter on a heavy gun or autocannon) and fires its own calibre through it; **every ammo fits every receiver** too (the receiver sets the calibre, the ammo picks the round type: hollow points, buckshot, slugs, armour piercing, frag, plasma... as relative changes on top), so only the magazine must match the receiver's rounds. Double-click a part in your parts to fit it to its best free socket, double-click a part of the build (in the list or on the gun) to take it off with what's on it (Undo puts it back), **Auto-complete** fills every empty needed socket, right-click (or drag off) a part to take it off, **Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y** undo and redo. Right-click a receiver > **Copy receiver assembly** duplicates it with everything on it onto a free receiver slot, even without the parts: **missing parts** show red on the gun, in the list and as a badge on saved builds and presets ("Missing: 1x Long Barrel, 1x Extended Mag"), and the build can't be saved or applied until you have them; clicking the greyed-out Save, Apply or Test fire button on an unfinished gun circles every empty socket it still needs in red on the model, hover one for what goes there, click it for those parts. Four build slots and the melee slot are saved in `workbench.txt`; the **Blueprints** section keeps any number of named builds (guns, melee, grenades) in `blueprints.txt`: save, load, save over, rename, duplicate, delete; in a Zombies run a blueprint loads with only the parts you own, the rest left as red-ringed empty sockets and listed under the gun; the **141 standard builds** (`Parts/Builds/`: 98 mystery box guns from uncommon to wonder, 23 wall guns (11 of them box guns too), 32 melee builds the slot machines pay) load as presets. Built guns have **no damage cap** a projectile (weapon files keep their 1000). Sandbox toggles: practice parts (a Zombies-like inventory, to try missing parts), weapon slots 3-4 (in Zombies an upgrade bought on the map, per run; the range also has its station) and practice dummies. **Test fire** opens the **Workbench Range**.
- New keys: **F** interact (cart, slot station) and **3** for weapon slot 3 (slot 4 is unbound by default); the swap key and mouse wheel cycle through every slot you have.
- Every barrel fires from its own muzzle, minigun receivers spin up, and one trigger pull counts as one hit per target (a Last Stand shield loses one charge, however many barrels hit). The standard weapons are unchanged.
- The design (component model, rules, stat pipeline, the user's decisions, rarity tiers, run progression and drop tables, the cart, the melee slot, example builds and the open questions) is in [docs/sandbox/WEAPON-BUILDER.md](../sandbox/WEAPON-BUILDER.md).

A pull that wins nothing frees the machine as soon as the reels stop, so you can pull again straight away; a part you already hold three of pays points back instead. The odds get rarer with the rounds, a machine **warms up** (its heat meter fills) with every pull that pays nothing rare, and every 5th round is a **jackpot round** with wonder parts on the reels. Every win is shown to everyone: the part turns over the machine in its rarity colour (a pillar of light for legendary and wonder) and a coloured line in the feed says who won what.
- **The trader** (2026-10-28): a wandering parts dealer sets up his stall at the start of **every 5th round** (5, 10, 15...) at one of the map's trader spots (a different one each visit), announced with a feed line, a sound, the banner THE TRADER IS HERE and a gold **TRADER 34 m** waypoint label through the walls (a thin beam over his stall too). He stays that round and the break after it, then leaves when the next round starts. He's where you **stock up on lots of lower-level parts for silly builds**: press F at his counter for his **shelf**, a fresh one every visit of 14 parts, one from each category (grenade parts too), mostly commons and uncommons (now and then one rare), plus a **grenade refill** (when you're short) and a **full reserve for every gun**. **No purchase limit**: buy anything as often as you like, **x1 / x5 / x10** at a time, priced by rarity (250 / 500 / 1000...) plus 3 percent a round. Bought parts go straight to your loose parts for the workbench. Zombies ignore him and nothing can hurt him; co-op bots buy one part a visit when they have the points to spare. Numbers in `Parts/Tables/ZombiesRules.json` "trader"; maps put `traderSpots` in their zombies section (without any he stands in front of a box spot or workbench). Protocol 73.
- On real maps the machines (perks, the box, workbenches, the power switch, the slot upgrade, the slot machines) are solid props. Every perk machine is generated by `tools/zombies/make_perk_machines.py` (Props/Zombies/PerkMachines).
- **Zombies sound alive**: you hear a horde coming and where from. Their **footsteps** follow their walk cycle (a walker's dragged shuffle, a runner's thud, a sprinter's stamp, heard 16 / 26 / 36 m away); each swipe starts with a **snarl** (the hit you take is a separate sound); a body shot gets a **grunt**, a head shot a **screech**, a kill a **death rattle** and a headshot kill a screech cut off into a gurgle; and they **groan, moan and growl** on their own, more often while chasing or tearing at boards, and growl as they climb in or rise from the ground. Every sound is synthesised (a throat model through formant filters, no samples), every zombie has its own voice pitch and never repeats its last variant, and it's all positional. Zombie voices have their own 8 voices and their steps another 8 (nearest first; an idle groan never takes a voice from a snarl or a death), with at most one voice per zombie and about 3 idle groans a second for the whole horde (up to 6 for a horde of hundreds), so even a 300-zombie round can't flood OpenAL or the gunfire's voices. Every PC works the sounds out from the zombies it already draws (no server work); the head-shot bit is the only new byte of state (protocol 50). Mixer rows: *Zombie voices* and *Zombie footsteps*, on top of *Zombies* (see Audio mixer).
- Co-op: built guns, dropped builds, power-ups, disassembled (empty) slots and the slot machines' reels, bets and wins replicate to every player (protocol 55). **Co-op bots spend their own points** like players when it's safe: the power, perks, better wall guns, the slot upgrade, the box, a door between rounds, the odd slot pull; reviving and boarding always come first, and they walk back to their post. ### Co-op (PvE)

**Co-op** is a player-versus-enemy mode (phase 1 of a bigger feature; the plan is in [docs/archive/PVE-DESIGN.md](PVE-DESIGN.md)). Pick **World Tour: Silo**, then the game mode **Co-op** (single player with bots, or host for friends). Your own loadouts, perks, grenades and launchers apply; kill streaks are off for now.

- **Silo Breach** (4 objectives): **breach the perimeter** (kill the enemies round the west portal), **plant charges on the cableway power** (stand at each of the three targets for a few seconds; the charge blows after a short fuse and takes nearby enemies with it), **hold launch control for 3 minutes** against escalating waves (a red loss meter fills while enemies stand in the room and none of you do), then **take the lift up and extract at the silo pad** (everyone in the green ring). The objective panel under the match bar shows progress, timers and an arrow; markers show targets and zones in the world.
- **Enemies are not players**: up to 80 soldiers alive at once (far past the 32 player slots): riflemen, shotgun rushers, snipers, LMG heavies, grenadiers who throw frags at anyone who stays put, and officers who sharpen everyone near them. They take cover, flank along the bots' nav graph and shoot back with real weapon spread.
- **A spawn director** keeps them coming instead of spawning everything at once: squads of 2-6 appear out of your sight (never within 22 m, never where any of you can see), ahead of you and round the objective, in waves that build up, peak and give you a breather; it eases off when the squad is taking a beating and recycles enemies left far behind.
- **Respawning**: dead players come back at the current checkpoint after 6-20 s (by difficulty). If the whole squad is down (a **wipe**), or a defend point is lost, the objective restarts at its checkpoint after a short regroup. Hard allows 5 wipes per mission and Insane 3; Easy and Normal are unlimited.
- **Difficulty** is the lobby's **Bot difficulty**: more enemies, harder hits, tighter aim and quicker reactions from Easy to Insane. The squad size raises the enemy count too (Silo Breach: 26 alive solo on Normal, 53 with four, 74 with four on Insane).
- **XP**: 20 per enemy kill (+10 for a headshot; 40% of it goes to the gun as weapon XP), 150 per objective, 1,000 for the mission, plus the usual match XP.
- Network: enemies near you arrive 20 times a second, interest-managed (13 bytes each, the nearest 56 within 150 m), about 6-12 KB/s per player with 80 alive (protocol 37). Missions are data files: `Maps/<Pack>/Missions/<Name>.json` (format and every objective type in the design doc).

**What to test with friends**: a full run of Silo Breach with 2-4 people (do the waves feel fair, do enemies ever pop in where you're looking, is the defend stage tense but winnable on Normal), a deliberate wipe (everyone back at the checkpoint?), late joiners, and the fps with 60-80 enemies in the defend stage.
