# Space stations

The station kit is a part kit generator, `space_station` (`Source/PartKit/Generators/SpaceStation*.cs`):

- `SpaceStationLook.cs`: the shared industrial look (palette, finishes, plated walls with bands, seams, ribs and windows, roof clutter, PDC turrets). Every station piece uses it, so stations share one style.
- `SpaceStationKit.cs`: the building blocks, each a call that makes one or more pieces: `Block` (a square block of decks: floor, roof, four walls, deck slabs round a lift core, rooms inside a pressure wall with a service gap), `Spine` (the truss between blocks, with its lift tube), `Arm` (a docking arm in pieces of about 20 m: walkway, ducts, feeds, gantry rails), `Cradle` (a berth's clamp bracket at the arm's side: a Docking Clamp Large whose box is set out beside the arm, and a boarding tube; nothing under the ship, Karl 2026-11-18: only the clamp arm holds it), `Gantry`, `Apron` (a freight deck with a lift table and a tunnel under it), `Band`, `Mast`, `TankFarm`, `Radiator` (a truss boom with tapered panels above and below, heat pipes glowing at the root), `Refinery` (an open cage of process plant: hopper under the ore chute, crusher drum, smelter, columns, gas spheres, fins).
- `SpaceStationHex.cs`: hexagonal blocks (`HexBlock`: floor, roof, the six walls as one piece, decks, lift core; a corner points east), `Pad` (a landing pad with a Docking Hatch and a Docking Clamp Medium), `SkyBridge`, `Fittings` (open floors' planters, bunk pods, stalls, beds, desks, tables). Their looks are hulls; their collision is hidden axis-aligned boxes, a staircase inside each slab and a run of steps inside each slanted wall, so pieces still meet face to face. The ring station uses its `FacePanel` and `Pad`; `HexBlock` and the square `Block` are unused since the third pass.
- `SpaceStationRing.cs`: the ring station (third pass, Karl 2026-11-18, after his reference picture `station/reference-ring-station.png` in the project files). `RingStation()` lays out Caledon Highport: a ring of 16 segments (apothem 108 to 132 m; pad, habitat, hangar, industry and tower kinds, each a stepped profile mitred to its neighbours under a joint collar) round one hexagonal hub with a faceted command dome and mast, three spokes, and a docking spindle under the hub: the customs drum, the dock collar (Magpie arms east and west), the freight collar (Dray arms east and west), the frigate collar (Zero Day arms north and south; one line of berths to a level, Karl 2026-11-18: "each z level can only have one line of ship docks", so ships come in along the line from the arms' ends), engineering with two radiator booms, the reactor and the drives. Looks are hulls in each piece's turned frame; collision is the `Lattice`: hidden boxes on one 2 m grid, each piece of a layer taking the cells its convex footprint holds, so pieces at any angle meet face to face and weld (`Reach` says where an arm welds on). About 47,000 triangles.
- `SpaceStationParts.cs`: `Make()` joins touching pieces with socket pairs (`Connect`, which throws on overlaps), orders them out from the hub, moves each origin to its piece's centre (`Recentre`: a box may reach at most 200 m from its origin) and writes the pieces to `Constructs/Parts/SpaceStations/Highport/` and the blueprint to `Constructs/Blueprints/CaledonHighport.json`. The first two passes (a 310 m tower, then a hex commons of seven towers) are in PR 301's history.

Pieces are in the `Station` group, unlisted in the Tab inventory and out of the Assembler (blueprint-only). Shaped looks are detail only and every piece says `"collider": "boxes"`. Berths are sized from the ships (`ZeroDay`, `Magpie`, `Dray` in `SpaceStationParts.cs`).

## Adding a station

Write a new plan method beside `RingStation()` with the kit's calls, call it from `Make()`, then `--gen-parts space_station`, `--check-vehicle Constructs/Blueprints/NAME.json` (the drive, mass and 220-part lines are a vehicle's and don't apply to a station; read the z-fighting ones) and `--selftest-only "No z-fighting, Compound collision"`.

The Magpie and Zero Day starts build Caledon Highport 40 km over Caledon (the opening's `"stations"`, `GameSpaceStart.OpeningStation`).

The chutes, conveyors, pipes, sky bridges and boarding tubes are looks only: nothing runs through them yet (no links). The clamps and hatches are the real parts: the clamps say `"startOn": true`, so every berth is armed from the start.

## In the game (`Game/GameStations.cs`)

- **A station** is any construct with a piece of the `Station` group (`Construct.IsStation`, from `ConstructPartDef.IsStationPiece`); its name is its pieces' section ("Caledon Highport").
- **The command view** lists it as a craft and draws it as a station: a ring round a hexagonal hub on three spokes with the spindle's arms past it, in lilac, with its name and "STATION  N OF M BERTHS FREE" (berths: its working docking ports). Its card says how to dock there. It takes no orders.
- **Berths moor a ship, they don't carry it** (Karl 2026-11-18: dock at its berths by hand and with the Dock order). A ship carried by a port can't carry craft itself, and the Zero Day carries its Shuttle and Limpet, so a station's berth holds a ship by mooring it: `Construct.MooredTo`, `MoorPart`, `MoorAt`, `MoorRot` (host only). Moored, it is set in the middle of the berth's box, upright with the clamp and its length along the box's, and each tick before the flight steps (`StationMoorStep`) its flight state is the station's plus the berth's offset, and its turn the station's, so the two go round together; its own thrust is ignored.
- **Docking:** the command view's Dock order on a station (it may carry craft; `NavDockCheck`) flies in as for any port and is moored where a ship would be caught (`NavDockInSpace`). **By hand:** six times a second the host looks for a ship in space that is mostly inside an armed, empty berth's box and slower than 3 m/s against it (`StationMoorScan`), and moors it.
- **Approach lanes** (Karl 2026-11-18: "there should be a landing path for each station, such that the way to get into each dock is as you'd expect"; `StationLanes`, `StationLaneAim`, `Construct.DockLanes`). Once per shape (`DockLanesFor` = PartCount) each berth is given one way out of six along its box's axes, tried in the order: for a long berth beside an arm its outer end (away from the station's middle), then up; for a square pad up, then an end; then the two short sides, down and the inner end: five rays from the box's face that way (the middle and near each corner) must clear the station out past its edge (`LaneClear`, 60 m); none clear, the one that goes farthest. The lane's mouth is out past the station's sphere along that axis. A Dock order at a station flies round the station's sphere (a point on a sphere 40 m past it when the straight way would cross it) to the mouth, then comes straight in down the lane under reaction jets at `NavDockLaneSpeed` (8 m/s), turning to the berth's mooring pose as it comes (`NavDockRcs`, at most 0.5 rad/s), and is moored in the box. **Berth classes go by size, not by ship** (Karl 2026-11-18: "the player will be making different sized ships, with each shipyard for a given size"; later corvette, frigate, destroyer, small and medium hauler berths): a berth takes only a ship that fits its box (its sizes, longest to shortest, each within 5% of the box's: `Game.BerthFits`), the snuggest free one first. A ship too big for every free berth gets "NO BERTH FITS" on the command view, and no Dock order, no mooring by hand (`StationMoorScan`) and no clamp arm (`Reachable`, `StationArmTake`) takes it. The lanes are kept open to stations that grow (more rings, more levels, shipyards in a row): they are worked out from the station's shape, so a new berth gets one on its first rebuild.
- **Casting off:** the pilot's key (as a clamp lets go), or any new order from the command view. A ship cast off isn't moored again by that berth until it has left its box (`MoorAway`).
- A station's berths never carry a craft (`ConstructCargo.Catchable` refuses a station carrier), so every ship there is moored, whatever it carries. A moored ship isn't levelled or steered by `SpacePose`, and once a second the host checks it still lies in its berth's box (parts are renumbered when others come off: `StationMoorRecheck` finds the berth again, else casts it off).
- **Not yet:** a save doesn't keep a mooring (the ship drifts off on load); in a world where power is required the clamps need power the station doesn't have.

## Clamp arms (`Constructs/ConstructClampArms.cs`, `Game/GameStationArms.cs`, `Render/ClampArmRender.cs`)

Karl, 2026-11-18: berths no longer have to match each ship's shape. A docking clamp comes out and takes the ship. When it touches, the ship stops any thrust and calculation and the arm pulls it smoothly into a fixed place, where nothing moves it. A shuttle released from a docked ship moves as normal. The look was agreed in the Station gallery (`Clamp Arm Demo`, a four-step storyboard blueprint).

- **The parts.** Each Highport berth has a clamp arm base on its docking arm's top, 6 m (Magpie: 4 m) along from the berth's middle toward its own side, its saddle 13 m (Magpie: 11 m) further out (`SpaceStationClampArm.cs`, `ClampArmBase`). The base's `"clampArm"` block gives its shoulder, where its wrist rests stowed and its reach (60 m), in the part's frame. The moving half is the parts `Station Clamp Arm Yoke`, `Boom 1` to `5` and the three heads (parked, seeking, holding) under `Constructs/Parts/SpaceStations/ClampArm`. They are never placed: `ClampArmRender` draws them where the arm points.
- **The rule** (every peer, `ConstructClampArms.Step`; the host each tick, a client each frame). An arm's berth is the dock part nearest its shoulder. It reaches for a ship that is slower than 4 m/s against the station, has its hull within reach, is nearest that berth of all the station's berths, and finds the berth free. It must also lie within 20 degrees of how it will sit in the berth (`LinedUp`, `LineUpMost`; Karl, 2026-11-18: the ship passes alongside, the clamp doesn't turn it), so the arm draws it in along the berth's line and never swings it round. Its head runs out at 8 m/s to the face of the ship's bounds that looks most at the arm (never the bow or stern), brought onto the hull by one ray. If the ship leaves or speeds up, the head goes back to its saddle.
- **Taking the ship** (host, `StationArmTake`). When the head touches, the ship's order ends (docked) and it is moored at the berth (`StationMoor`). At a berth with an arm it is drawn in from where it lay, eased, at 3 m/s (3 to 20 s; `MoorFrom*`, `MoorDraw`). The berth's box check (`StationMoorRecheck`) waits until it is in. A moored ship is posed from the station each tick; its own thrust and flight don't move it. A ship moored any other way (the Dock order's own catch, or flown into the box by hand) is drawn in the same way.
- **Where the pad goes** (`Grip`): on the ship's Docking Hatch (a door part with `"dock": true`) nearest the arm, of those facing it (Karl 2026-11-18: a docking connector on top for the arm, on any ship); with none facing it, the middle of the face of the ship's bounds that looks most at the arm, brought onto the hull by one ray.
- **Clients** step the same rule from the poses they see, so their arms reach when the host's do; the hold follows the moorings in `Msg.Stations`.
- **Not yet:** arms don't collide with anything; a client's arm can lag its host's by a snapshot.

## Markets (`Game/GameStationMarket.cs`, `Crew/Stations.json`, the card in `UI/UiCommandStation.cs`)

Karl, 2026-11-18: "The first gameplay loop is being able to dock with it and sell mined ores", and "as a logistics point, it should show up on the friendly market as whatever goods are there to trade. Haulers from other neutral factions can then send ships to buy goods and take them back to their station."

- **Data:** `Crew/Stations.json` (`call-of-slop-stations/1`) names each station's market by the station's name: the power holding it (`"power"`, Caledon Highport: the Traders), its planet, and its goods (`"good"`, a material of `Constructs/Recycling.json`; `"buy"` and `"sell"`, value a unit on a tab; `"stock"` at the start; `"most"` it holds). `"margin"`: the share of what a station pays that its power counts in its stock (`PowerControllers.Power.Stock` and `Earned`, so its books still add up).
- **The host** makes a station's market the first time it sees it (`StationMarketOf`), keeps its stock, and once a second notes who is moored at it; `Msg.Stations` (protocol 361, the slot of `RemovedDepot`) sends the markets and moorings to everyone when they change (at most once a second) and to each joiner, and a client sets `MooredTo` on its own constructs from it.
- **The card:** picking a station in the command view shows its card in place of a ship's: where it is, its berths, who holds it and whether they trade with you (a power whose standing with you is below 0 doesn't), and the market (each good's stock, what it pays, what it asks; FULL when it holds all it will). Aboard a ship moored there (standing on it or seated in it), TRADE CARGO says what the station would put on your tab for what that ship's bins hold and trades it (`FactionOp.StationSell`): every bin's material the station buys goes, up to its room, for value on your tab with its power; the notice lists what was traded.
- **Trying it:** `--window-shot X.png --map Caledon --start Magpie --survival --do "skip; wait 120; fill Storage Volume@b1 3000 iron ore; command 90000; cmdpick 1; order dock 2; wait 25000; stationsell; wait 90; market"` then `stationbuy 10; wait 90; stationsellpart 10; wait 90; market` for a part (`market` prints each market as this peer has it, its parts, and what the powers owe the player; `stationsell` sends what SELL CARGO sends for the picked craft).
- **Buying** (protocol 362, `FactionOp.StationBuy`): aboard a ship moored at a station, each good on its card has a BUY button, which fills the ship's bins (those holding that good first, then empty ones) with as much as the station has, the bins hold and your tab with its power pays for at its asking price. `stationbuy K` (the window tool) buys the Kth good for the picked craft.
- **Barter and the tab** (Karl, 2026-11-20: no currency, option C of the "Trade without currency" study; `Game/GameTabs.cs`): there are no credits. What a station pays goes on your tab with the power that holds it ("the Traders owe you 359"), spendable only with that power; what it asks comes off it. A power at war with you (standing at or below the war line) or fallen wipes its tab. The tab line is on the HUD and in the Faction window's Crew section; the card shows YOUR TAB with the station's power. Creative: unlimited.
- **Parts** (protocol 389): a station's `"parts"` list (`{"part": NAME, "stock", "most", "buy"?, "sell"?}`) trades construct parts, which are physical: BUY on a part's row puts one into a Storage Volume of the moored ship you are aboard, SELL trades every one the ship's stores hold, up to the station's room (`FactionOp.StationSellPart`). A part's price is its value (`TradeValues.Part`: its Assembling recipe's materials at the file's `"values"` and the parts in it at theirs, times `"work"`) times `"partBuy"` or `"partSell"`, unless its row says. A market's stock runs over its goods, then its parts (`Msg.Stations`).
- **Needs** (Karl, 2026-11-20: "incorporating needs to the world"): a good's `"uses"` and `"makes"` (units an hour of play) take from and add to a station's stock once a minute, inside 0 and its most, whether its world is being played or not (`Game.StationNeedsTick`; the host only, the stock goes out in `Msg.Stations`). `"want"` is the stock it aims for (default half its most). Prices follow the stock (`StationMarket.PriceFactor`: as listed at the want, up to 1.6 times as it runs out, down to 0.6 times at twice the want), worked out by both sides from the stock, so no price is on the wire. The card shows MAKES and USES, and SHORT beside a used good under half its want.
- **The Traders' stations** (phase 2 of the trade world): Caledon Highport (their hub; uses hydrogen, helium, steel, circuits), Cinder Fuel Depot (40 km over Cinder; makes hydrogen, helium, oxidiser) and Slopolis Exchange (25 km over Slopolis; makes circuits from copper and gold ore), both built from the Highport's blueprint as placeholders. Three Traders hauler routes join them (and Glacier Watch); their ships are placeholders (the Magpie): a route's `"ship"` swaps one in.
- **A second station: Glacier Watch** (Karl, 2026-11-18: "at a fixed place in another neutral faction's turf", over Glacier "to test faction areas", "more complicated pathfinding" and "how fuel scales"): the Militia's, built from the Highport's blueprint for now. A station whose `Crew/Stations.json` entry has a `"blueprint"` is built in its planet's world, `"orbit"` km up over the map's middle on a circular orbit, the first time that world is played without it (`Game.StationsBuildTick`), and stays with that world after (a landing keeps it in the planet's folder). Its name is its entry's (`Construct.StationTitle`; a client takes it from the market's name). The Militia pay more for ore than the Highport and sell steel cheaper. A start's `"stations"` entry may give a `"name"` too.
- **Arriving in orbit** (`GameSpaceLanding.cs`): a ship with a player seated aboard comes into a planet's world out at its station's orbit and 20 km past it (`StationReach`), not only at the sky's edge, so the group arrives in orbit beside Glacier Watch and can dock without going down first. A ship that can't arrive there (someone not aboard) is held only at the sky's edge, as before; ships nobody sits in go by the sky's edge.
- **Markets across worlds:** the host keeps each station's market by its entry (`MarketOfDef`), so a landing keeps every station's stock; a new game starts them from the data.
- **Haulers** (`Game/GameStationHaulers.cs`; Karl: "Haulers from other neutral factions can then send ships to buy goods and take them back to their station"): `Crew/Stations.json`'s `"haulers"` routes. Each keeps `"count"` real ships (`"ship"`, crewed by `"power"`): one sets out from whichever of its stations is in the world being played, a kilometre and a half behind it, and a lost one is replaced a minute later. A hauler bound for a station in this world closes on it on a circle 8 km under it (over it, when the station is behind), then docks with the Dock order from 6 km off (a Dock order flown straight across 40 km of orbit burned a Magpie's whole tank). One bound for a station in another world flies an Orbit order to that station's height round its planet (the trip planner between planets, on its own fuel) and trades once on that circle. At each end it sells what it carries that the station buys, fills its tanks, buys the route's `"goods"` (or `"back"` at the far end; paid from its power's stock, the station's power counting its margin), waits `"wait"` seconds and sets off. Its command-view card says HAULING TO or TRADING AT (on the host). The first route: a Traders Magpie carrying iron and copper ore from the Highport to Glacier Watch and steel back. `[haul]` lines in the log follow each leg and trade.
- **Convoys and supply by need** (phase 3 of the trade world): a route's `"escort"` (`{ "ship", "count" up to 4, "gap" }`) puts warships of its power with each hauler (`EscortRun`, `HaulEscort` in the save: the route's key valued 2 and up). Out on a leg an escort holds `"gap"` m beside its hauler with a Meet order (between worlds too, on the trip planner); while the hauler closes on, docks at or lies moored at a station in this world, it holds over the station (`NavOrder.MeetAbove`: along the station's Y, out of the berths' lanes, which run along its X) three gaps clear. A hauler waits up to two minutes past its `"wait"` for its escorts to gather within 5 km before a leg; its station fills the escorts' tanks with its own; a lost escort is replaced at the hauler's next station here a minute on, and one that lost its hauler joins the route's next. `"needs": true` loads, besides the route's goods, any good the far station is short of (under its want) that this one sells, most needed first; a listed good the far one is full of stays, and under 50 units of a good isn't loaded. The placeholder escort is the Traders' Zero Day. Docking: a berth another ship's Dock order is flying to is passed over while another fits (`NavDockFor` with the orders; three haulers once picked the same Magpie berth and knocked one another kilometres off).
- **Crews under orders:** a crewed ship under a command-view order (or a hauler's leg) with nothing to fight leaves the helm to its flight computer (`ICrewHost.UnderOrders`): the crew's pilot held its place against the autopilot before.
- **Not yet:** prices that move with stock; a market's stock, a mooring, a station's own name or a hauler's route kept in a save (they start again from the data, and a loaded world's haulers are plain crewed ships while new ones set out); free travel (a friend alone on Glacier has that world's own markets).
- **Trying it:** `--window-shot X.png --map Caledon --start ZeroDay --survival --do "skip; wait 120; command 90000; cmdpick 1; order dock 3; wait 19000; nav"` (the Zero Day climbs 37 km and moors in about five minutes); `moorclear` (host) lets a ship cast off be moored again by the berth it lies in.
