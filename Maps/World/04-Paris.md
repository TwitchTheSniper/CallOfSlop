# 04 Paris Quarter (medium, 16 players)

A hillside Paris quarter in the late afternoon (`clear` sky, low sun at Paris's latitude, warm haze), built from three
new reusable buildings, the new `Props/Parisian` street furniture and French signage. Generated by
`tools/world/paris.py` (map), `tools/world/paris_buildings.py` (buildings) and `tools/world/paris_assets.py` (props,
building fittings, decals); run them from the repository root.

## Layout

Mirror-symmetric about x = 0: Blue holds the west plaza, Red the east plaza. Every building, block, prop and decal on
one half has a twin at (-x, z) (buildings turned and mirrored), so both teams get the same walk to every objective.

The quarter climbs from south to north in three levels joined by stone retaining walls with stairs:

- **A, Rue du Moulin (y 0).** The south street and the south row: three Haussmann apartment blocks and a boulangerie
  per side, their back courtyards (garden walls with gateways, bins, bikes, washing lines, trees) and two dead-end
  alleys and an impasse poking out of the outline.
- **B, Place de la Fontaine (y 3).** The raised square with the tiered fountain (hill 1), plane trees, benches, a cafe
  terrace, a kiosk, a flower stall and an advertising column; an island apartment block on each side (its shop is the
  Petit Zinc cafe-tabac); the team plazas at both ends with a cul-de-sac (the flags) and the **metro**: a railed
  trench with a cast-iron arch in each plaza drops into a zig-zag tunnel and a station hall under the square
  (pillars, benches, ticket and vending machines, posters). It is the underground flank between the plazas.
- **C, Rue des Lilas (y 6).** The north street and the north row: two Haussmann blocks, a boulangerie and a corner
  cafe-brasserie per side; the two brasseries face each other across the Passage des Artistes (terraces, and a plank
  sky bridge between their roofs). Behind them the north courtyards, an alley out to a dead end, and the belvedere
  (y 9) up a flight of steps at the top of the passage, looking out at a domed basilica on the butte.

Grand stairs (8 m) join the levels in the middle (hills 2 and 3 sit on Rue des Lilas and Rue du Moulin in front of
them), side stairs at x = +-24, and steep cobbled ramps (Rue de la Butte) climb from each plaza to both streets.

**Verticality.** Every Haussmann block is six storeys round a stone stairwell: shop and entrance hall, four floors
of flats (salon, bedroom, kitchen), a balcony along the second floor and the attic behind a parapeted terrace on the
fifth. Terraces of neighbouring blocks join across alleys on planks (with steps up to the parapets), the south blocks'
terraces join over their party walls, and the brasseries and boulangeries have stairs onto flat roof terraces.

**Outline.** A polygon, no straight box: the alleys, the impasse, the belvedere and the plaza cul-de-sacs jut out.
Beyond it a ring of limestone facades with windows and zinc roofs closes every view; past the belvedere, the basilica.

## New assets

- Buildings (`Buildings/`): **Haussmann Apartment** (6 floors, 660 boxes), **Cafe Brasserie** (corner, 3 floors,
  roof terrace, zinc bar, 392 boxes), **Boulangerie** (2 floors, bakehouse, roof terrace, 187 boxes), each with palettes;
  shown on the Dev pack's **Paris Street** (`Maps/Dev/BuildingsParis.json`). Stair landings are at least 1.15 m.
- Props (`Props/Parisian`, general): Bistro Table, Bistro Chair, Cafe Parasol, Advertising Column, Cast Iron Drinking
  Fountain, Tiered Square Fountain, News Kiosk, Metro Railing, Metro Entrance Arch, Lantern Street Lamp, Cast Iron
  Bollard, Flower Stall, Chimney Pot Stack, Window Flower Box, Zinc Bar Counter, Bar Bottle Shelf. Building-own props:
  bread counter, bread rack, deck oven, bakers' table (Boulangerie); banquette, menu chalkboard (Cafe Brasserie).
- Decals: global `Decals/Signs` (French trade lettering: boulangerie, patisserie, brasserie, cafe, tabac, pharmacie,
  librairie, fromagerie, metro; a cycle-lane symbol; the LIVRAISON bay word) and pack-local `Maps/World/Decals/Paris`
  (eight invented shop names, four blue enamel street plaques).
